    Solving it: Gauss elimination vs Gauss-Seidel

    Module I · CO1 · direct & iterative methods

    Gauss elimination solves it exactly in two moves. The iterative methods creep up on the answer; Gauss-Seidel uses each new value straight away, so it gets there about twice as fast as Jacobi.

      The loss surface: partial derivatives & the minimum test

      Module III · CO3 · maxima, minima & eigenvalues

      The loss is a bowl. Its second derivatives pass the minimum test, and the eigenvalues of the Hessian show it is a long, narrow valley: steep one way, flat the other. The chain rule that finds the slope is exactly backpropagation.

        Constrained training: Lagrange multipliers

        Module III · CO3 · ridge regression

        Give the weights a budget: they must lie on a circle of radius s. The best point is where a loss contour just touches the circle, and there the slope points straight along the weights. This is ridge regression, a standard way to stop AI weights growing too large.

        ∇L+μw=0⇒(A+μI)w=b,w12+w22=s2

          Training as a differential equation

          Module II · CO2 · linear ODE systems, stability, Euler

          With tiny steps, training follows a system of linear ODEs. Diagonalising A splits it into two exponential decays, one fast and one slow. Ordinary gradient descent is just Euler's method on this ODE with step h = η, and the loss never goes up along the exact flow.

            The learning-rate limit

            Modules I + II · CO6 · the headline result

            In eigen-coordinates every step multiplies each error mode by its own factor. Training converges only if every factor stays inside (−1, 1). The eigenvalues predicted the limit before any training ran, and all seven runs agreed.
